Why it matters

The most powerful AIs are owned
by a handful of companies.

They decide what AI does and what it costs, and they keep what it earns. AQL flips the model: the AI belongs to the people who keep it running.

Today's model

  • One company owns the AI and the servers that run it.
  • What the AI earns goes to its shareholders.
  • The rules change whenever it decides.
  • You can't see anything that happens inside.

The AQL model

  • Independent machines around the world run it.
  • 67% of every payment goes to those who serve it, 33% to those who hold AQL.
  • The rules live in a contract no one can change.
  • The code, the tests and every payment are public.

01 — Serve

Independent machines, not a datacenter

Every request is handled by a miner running the network's model on their own machine.

02 — Verify

Cheating doesn't pay

Some answers are replayed by another miner; if they disagree, a third one settles it. Whoever diverges loses their earnings and their reputation.

03 — Improve

It gets better on its own

Miners propose better versions. Only the ones that score higher on a public test, measured by other miners, are adopted.

Emission

Early matters.

Half of all AQL is issued in the first two years. After that, the daily reward is cut in half every two years. The schedule follows the chain's clock: no one can speed it up.

The earlier you bring useful compute, the more AQL you accumulate, and the bigger your share of payments once you stake it.
